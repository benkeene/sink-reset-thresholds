# Claim claim_0001: Sharp reset thresholds for bounded-response synchronization games with a sink

**Problem:** finite-state-dynamics-and-automata--sink-adversarial-reset-threshold

**Statement.** M(5,2) = 6: every 5-state A_2 automaton with a sink has a reset word of length at most 6, and 6 is attained. More fully, M(n,k) = sum_{i=1}^{n-1} ceil(i/k) for every 3 <= n <= 5 and 1 <= k <= n-1, which includes the non-divisible cases (4,2) and (5,3) that test the stronger ceiling-sum form of the conjecture.

**Definitions.** A DFA on states Q = {0, ..., n-1} with a finite alphabet of letters, each a transformation of Q; state 0 is a sink (fixed by every letter). In the k-game (Lipin-Volkov, arXiv:2601.18362, section 2.1) Alice plays one letter and Bob replies with any word of length strictly less than k, including the empty word; Alice wins when the history is a reset word (brings every state to one state, necessarily 0). The automaton is A_k when Alice has a winning strategy; who moves first does not matter. The reset threshold rt is the length of a shortest reset word. M(n,k) is the maximum of rt over all n-state A_k automata with a sink, over every finite alphabet.

**Context.** Lipin and Volkov prove M(n,1) = n(n-1)/2 and M(n,n-1) = n-1, exhibit the A_k automaton L_m^k (n = k+m+1 states) with rt = sum_{i=1}^{n-1} ceil(i/k), and conjecture that (n-1+k)(n-1)/(2k) is the value of M(n,k) whenever k divides n-1. The case (5,2) is the first with k dividing n-1 that is neither settled endpoint, so the first on which the conjecture itself is open ((4,2) is the first non-endpoint pair of all; erratum applied 2026-10-30 after the Palomar review of 517b2cd, see README). The statement above is the finite case of that conjecture, in its ceiling-sum form, for 3 <= n <= 5; no resolution of the general conjecture was located in the inspected sources (arXiv:2601.18362v1, §§2.1 and 3.4; the CiE 2026 chapter, doi:10.1007/978-3-032-31348-5_24, was not inspected in full text). An earlier exhaustive computation for $3\le n\le5$ corroborates the statement. Its artifacts, hashes and reproduction command are recorded in PROOF.md Appendix A (and in COMPUTATION.md once item 4 is applied), and its methodology audit in EVIDENCE.md. The statement is proved by hand in PROOF.md, which uses no computation.
